# Sydney Environmental Atlas · first integrated release

This project extends the existing Sydney Sound Atlas into one map with three independently sourced environmental lenses. The three lenses share the same selected point, search, suburb shortcuts, map navigation and A/B comparison. They **must not** be collapsed into a generic safety, quietness or livability score.

## Experience contract

1. Select a point on the map or search for an address. The selected place is retained while changing lenses.
2. Select **Noise**, **Heat**, or **Contamination** in the same prominent navigation control. Each lens changes the map symbology, legend, inspector and evidence ledger.
3. Compare two places in any lens. Switching lenses keeps the two comparison points and updates the compared values asynchronously.
4. Each result says what it is, its provenance, and what it is not. An unavailable record is never interpreted as low exposure or safety.
5. Markers and visual contours must be based on source geometry. No arbitrary aggregate risk scores, decibels or temperature readings.

### Human-first interface contract (v0.6)

- **One place, one surface:** the left rail contains search, selected location, three lens choices and the active observation. Avoid scattering core navigation and results across unrelated overlays.
- **Read the map in one glance:** all three lens choices show a *labelled* value for the same selected point when available: `48 m to road`, `31% tree cover`, `5 EPA sites / 1 km`. These are deliberately heterogeneous facts, not comparable grades. Preserve units, denominators and unavailable states.
- **The result precedes the caveat:** show the concrete reading first and one short interpretation afterwards. Put technical provenance, calculation details, long warnings and archival metadata behind an intentional “How do we know?” or detail disclosure. Never force the visitor through documentation before they can understand the place.
- **Map layers should look like their evidence:** road centrelines must look like lines, not a faux acoustic heatmap; canopy values must map to actual source hexagons; EPA sites to source points, not invented polluted parcels.
- **A small screen is a first-class view:** search remains at the top, location/lenses live in a bottom sheet and the selected pin remains visible on the map. Avoid a screen-filling explanatory dashboard.
- **No invented clarity:** if any source is still loading or unavailable, say so in that lens tile. A missing record is not a zero-exposure claim.

### Full-map 3D perspective, individual trees and daylight controls (v0.9)

- **Noise orientation:** the selected point's nearest mapped road is indicated on an 8-point compass by actual geospatial bearing. This is a *direction to a road*, not a loudest façade, an acoustic propagation direction or a measured decibel level. The previous decorative “THE DISTANCE ON THE MAP” heading is removed.
- **Solar position:** Heat adds an in-place Sydney date and local-time control (05:00–20:00, 10-minute steps) and a solar elevation/azimuth computation based on the NOAA solar-geometry equations. The chosen pin is the calculation location. Sydney's UTC+10/UTC+11 offset comes from the browser's `Australia/Sydney` time-zone database.
- **The map itself changes perspective:** selecting Noise or Heat prefers a tilted, rotatable MapLibre GL map of the *same georeferenced neighbourhood*, with road lines, a selected point and an OpenStreetMap-derived building extrusion layer where height attributes are available. Visitors can switch to 2D. Contamination retains the verified 2D EPA point layer. Where WebGL is unavailable the verified 2D map remains accessible.
- **Daylight on the actual map:** Heat displays a gold ray from the selected point in the calculated sun bearing. In 3D, it now adds ground-plane shadow polygons cast away from the sun by visible OpenStreetMap building footprints with available `render_height`, plus time-dependent directional lighting on the extruded buildings. The sun bearing ray is still a direction cue, not a physical ray length. The earlier artificial 10 m pole and illustration stay removed.
- **Measured individual tree dimensions in a limited area:** City of Sydney's [published park/street tree asset register](https://services1.arcgis.com/cNVyNtjGVZybOQWZ/ArcGIS/rest/services/Trees/FeatureServer/0) supplies individual points, `TreeHeight` and `TreeCanopyNS`. The client queries its current viewport and draws up to 350 registered trees with both plausible dimensions; the shape is a five-part **cartographic schematic**, not a surveyed crown or shadow. Kensington/Randwick council has no verified equivalent tree register integrated. The rest of Sydney continues to show source H3 canopy aggregates **without inventing individual trees**.
- **Actual terrain and local surface elevation:** in the full 3D map, [Mapterhorn](https://mapterhorn.com/) Terrarium-encoded tiles deform the ground at nominal exaggeration 1. Their underlying DEM resolution/age varies, with attribution published by Mapterhorn; this is not the NSW 5 m raster. For the selected point in Heat, the app independently queries the [NSW Spatial Services 5 m elevation ImageServer](https://maps.six.nsw.gov.au/arcgis/rest/services/public/NSW_5M_Elevation/ImageServer) and shows the measured/modelled raster pixel height only if numeric; API errors or `NoData` remain absent.
- **Honest source separation:** building extrusions and ground shadows use OpenStreetMap-derived `render_height` values, **not surveyed heights**. The shadow algorithm projects footprint vertices by `height / tan(solar elevation)` in the opposite azimuth and sweeps exterior edges; it still assumes **level ground even when the 3D display shows DEM terrain**, omits roofs with nonzero `render_min_height`, geometries with missing heights, shadows longer than 220 m, solar elevation ≤6°, tree shadows, and unobserved obstruction. At most 250 mapped buildings are processed per camera view. The visualisation is a **neighbourhood estimate, not validated property-level sunlight access**. Directional hourly sound predictions are **not available**. Do not present the view as a home's actual sun/noise exposure.

### Lens scale and on-map interpretation (v0.10)

- **Noise on the map:** the 200 m radius and the nearest-road connection are now rendered within the 3D neighbourhood, with a derived metre-distance label; 2D highlights the nearest centreline endpoint and thickens the connector. These are straight-line GIS geometries and not acoustic propagation, measured decibels or a source-specific noise level.
- **Land records explained:** the label and inspector explicitly explain that this lens locates **NSW EPA notifications of potentially contaminated sites** within 1 km. The tan/rust points represent notification locations, not contaminated parcels, boundaries or health exposure. A 1 km ring and a restore-overview button make the query area discoverable.
- **Mode-aware camera:** switching lenses only zooms **out** when the previous camera is too close to convey the new source extent: roughly zoom 16.7 (Noise), 16.8 (Heat), or 14.35 (Land) on desktop, with more room on mobile. The original selected pin stays the destination; the app never silently zooms in just because a mode changes. Explicit 2D/3D switching also preserves the prior viewpoint.
- **No disappearing high-zoom 2D map:** GA's ArcGIS cached map advertises high LOD metadata, but tested Sydney z17, z18 and z19 raster URLs returned 404. Leaflet now uses `maxNativeZoom: 16` to safely enlarge the last available actual imagery when visitors zoom farther. At high zoom the basemap is less sharp, but it is no longer empty. A briefly considered third-party street tile fallback was removed after the test environment received policy-related 403 tiles.

#### Path toward actual 2.5D neighbourhood sunlight, noise and works

1. Obtain georeferenced footprints **and licensed height geometry** for buildings; obtain terrain surfaces (e.g. NSW LiDAR/DEM) and vegetation height/canopy geometry with dates and provenances. Do not assume generic storey heights are factual heights. Not all NSW Geoscape building data is licensed for public redistribution.
2. Advance the preliminary OSM-height, level-ground shadow projection to *surveyed and licensed* geometry. Handle sloping terrain, overhangs, missing heights, dynamic development states, seasonal trees, camera tilt and obstruction. Validate against field observations and shadow studies before claiming property-level fidelity.
3. Integrate the NSW Planning Portal's daily-updated DA feed or verified planning spatial service, preserving identifiers, status, date, official source, proposal footprint and distinction between *lodged/approved/proposed* and *actively under construction*. Approved DA does not imply a work site is operating.
4. Add road/rail/aircraft acoustic inputs, hourly traffic mix/speed, construction activity schedules, barriers, façade receiver points and calibrated measured validation. Only then label directional contours or hourly dB as **modelled sound**, with error bounds and source age. Until then, keep road bearing clearly a geometry observation.
5. Use a common time axis for sun and sound once acoustic temporal inputs are validated, but allow each layer to report its own historical snapshot, confidence and unavailable state. Nighttime construction or future building height must not be inferred solely from a DA record.

## Sources and metrics

| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Noise | NSW Spatial Services road centreline snapshot, with OpenStreetMap fallback outside snapshot bounds | Nearest road centreline distance and bearing; 200 m geometry circle and road geometry on map | Actual dB, daily/night traffic volumes, rail/flight noise, indoor or bedroom conditions |
| Heat | [NSW Government 2024–25 canopy access H3 grid](https://mapprod2.environment.nsw.gov.au/arcgis/rest/services/UHGC/GSR_2024_VegStrata_H3Z11/MapServer/0); [NOAA solar geometry](https://gml.noaa.gov/grad/solcalc/); OpenStreetMap building heights; [City of Sydney tree register](https://services1.arcgis.com/cNVyNtjGVZybOQWZ/ArcGIS/rest/services/Trees/FeatureServer/0); [Mapterhorn terrain](https://mapterhorn.com/); [NSW 5m DEM](https://maps.six.nsw.gov.au/arcgis/rest/services/public/NSW_5M_Elevation/ImageServer) | `Canopy_pc` in selected hex; mapped H3 polygons; sun bearing/elevation; flat-ground approximate building shadows; in City of Sydney only, schematic 3D individual trees from registered heights/spreads; physical 3D terrain relief and a separate NSW DEM height at pin if available | Accurate complete tree positions elsewhere, terrain-corrected building/tree shadows, surface/air temperature, indoor heat, directly measured heat island intensity |
| Contamination | [NSW EPA notified sites FeatureServer](https://mapprod2.environment.nsw.gov.au/arcgis/rest/services/EPA/Contaminated_land_notified_sites/FeatureServer/0) | Count of EPA site points within 1 km, nearest notified site, its management class, official record date, markers on map | Site perimeter, exposure route, contaminant concentration, site-specific testing, absence of contamination |

Each live environmental layer is fetched **directly by the visitor's browser** from the public government GIS service. We do not bundle/re-publish the underlying full dataset; the service may be temporarily unavailable. These are source records, not continuously measured conditions. Official sources, limitation notes and attribution are presented in the map and Evidence drawer.

The 2D base map uses [Geoscience Australia's NationalBaseMap cached raster tiles](https://services.ga.gov.au/gis/rest/services/NationalBaseMap/MapServer), licensed under CC BY 4.0, with OpenStreetMap contributor attribution. The perspective map uses [MapLibre GL JS](https://maplibre.org/maplibre-gl-js/) and [OpenFreeMap](https://openfreemap.org/) vector tiles for the base and building geometry, retaining their map attribution; physical terrain is tiled by [Mapterhorn](https://mapterhorn.com/attribution/) and credited through the style. Heights may be incomplete. The EPA service is published under CC BY 4.0. [City of Sydney open-data policy](https://www.cityofsydney.nsw.gov.au/library-information-services/access-open-data) permits reuse subject to hub disclaimers. The [canopy-access data catalogue](https://data.nsw.gov.au/data/dataset/greater-sydney-region-tree-canopy-access-202425) lists an OEH licence; confirm terms before commercial data reuse or repackaging.

## Behaviour and scope limits

- The heat grid is a local ~hexagonal canopy layer around the selected point, rather than a heat interpolation over an entire suburb. An average for larger boundaries must come from official published aggregate statistics instead of averaging sampled hexagons.
- EPA counts use great-circle distance from a selected point to published site **point** features within 1 km; no spatial claim about a site's boundary or migration of contamination is made. The list is not exhaustive.
- The noisy-road layer currently uses road geometry, not measured emissions or receiver-level exposure. Road distance is an inspectable geometric fact, not a modelled sound score.
- External source errors are shown clearly with unavailable states; never silently replace missing observations with a synthetic number.
- GeoJSON query results may be capped. The current environmental queries reject capped responses rather than display partial counts as complete.

## Next evidence milestones

1. **Noise:** investigate official transport traffic volumes, aircraft/rail contours, calibrated measurements and a location-specific nighttime model with evaluation before introducing any sound predictions.
2. **Heat:** independently licensed thermal measurements or the Greater Sydney 2022 Heat Vulnerability Index, clearly distinguished from the 2024–25 tree-cover grids and from indoor temperature.
3. **Land:** surface official regulated-land notice documents, site histories and appropriate parcel lookup; preserve the difference between notified, regulated, investigated, remediated and unknown.
4. **Trust and UX:** add explicit source timestamps for all layers, downloadable location evidence snapshots, accessible mobile comparison, and user testing of whether viewers correctly understand each layer's limits.

## Smoke tests

- Kensington → Heat: the NSW canopy service returns a containing hexagon and a numeric canopy percentage; map renders coloured hexagons.
- Kensington → Contamination: the NSW EPA service returns published nearby notifications with names and management classes; map shows individual dots.
- Kensington → Coogee: save first place, select second, and verify both compare values arrive in the active lens; switch lenses and verify comparison updates.
- Noise: road distance retains existing geometry semantics; no `dB` or fabricated sound score.
- 3D: on a WebGL-enabled browser, Noise or Heat must **tilt the full map viewport** and show buildings where sourced render-height data exists. Check pan, rotate, zoom, point selection and explicit 2D switch. On unsupported hardware ensure the 2D map still works and never claims 3D is active.
- 3D regression: MapLibre GL JS **v6 does not export `supported()`**. Never use `!maplibregl.supported?.()` as a WebGL feature test; that incorrectly disables 3D on every device. Verify the `Map` constructor exists, attempt real initialization, and handle actual WebGL2/GPU failures. The managed browser can lack WebGL2; this does not prove a user's desktop Chrome lacks it.
- Heat solar: 25 Oct 2026 at Sydney noon shows a high northern sun, while 21 Jun at noon shows a lower northern sun; their bearing indicator updates as the date/time control moves. At sun elevation below zero, no direct-sun ray is drawn. Moving the pin recomputes solar position, and switching away from Heat removes the ray and ground-shadow polygons.
- Heat 3D shadow projection: `node --test scripts/shadow-geometry.test.cjs` verifies azimuth orientation, metre-scale projection, missing or unsupported heights, duplicate ID handling, geometry rings and caps. On a WebGL2 browser, confirm the daylight slider changes the footprint-based ground shadow direction and apparent length, and leaves the rest of the map interactive. Low sun and buildings without source heights must **not** produce invented shadows. The managed preview browser does not support WebGL2, so a browser with actual WebGL2 is required for visual 3D sign-off.
- 3D trees: `node --test scripts/tree-geometry.test.cjs` checks observed `TreeHeight` and `TreeCanopyNS`, rejects missing/out-of-range dimensions, caps displayed trees, and produces closed rings. On a real WebGL2 browser, navigate to CBD or Pyrmont to verify tree representations and source-dimension click popups. Kensington must not silently fabricate tree positions/heights.
- DEM: in Heat, Kensington DEM lookup gives a numeric value or stays omitted on `NoData`; changing location must not show the previous point's height. Mapterhorn 3D relief must render without CORS failures on a real WebGL2 browser. Confirm overlays remain correctly positioned; the flat-shadow approximation must remain explicitly labelled.
- Verify failure cases for external services and inspect mobile layout before each release.
- Lens transition regression: zoom deep into Noise, switch to Land and verify a smooth *zoom out* to the entire 1 km query ring; zoom into Land and press **Show the full 1 km area** to restore context. Verify the selected point is preserved and there is no automatic zoom *in* when changing modes from a wide view.
- Tile coverage regression: inspect the 2D map at zoom 17–19; it must show enlarged GA imagery beneath source overlays, never the blank 404 tile grid. In a WebGL2-enabled browser, confirm the 3D Noise radius, nearest-road connector and distance label render above buildings without falsely suggesting acoustic intensity, and verify the 3D camera cannot zoom beyond its supported 18.5 cap.
